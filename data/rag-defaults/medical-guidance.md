# headache
Headaches can have many causes, so start with simple supportive care and watch for warning signs.
- Rest somewhere comfortable and drink some water.
- Avoid strenuous activity for now and monitor whether the headache improves.
Follow-up: How severe is the headache, and is it getting worse or coming with vomiting, vision changes, confusion, weakness, or fainting?

# dizziness
Dizziness can have many causes. The immediate priority is keeping yourself safe from a fall.
- Sit or lie down somewhere safe and avoid standing up suddenly.
- If you can drink normally, take small amounts of water and monitor how you feel.
Follow-up: Did the dizziness start suddenly, or are you having vomiting, vision changes, confusion, weakness, or fainting?

# nausea
Nausea can have many causes, so keep things simple while you see how you feel.
- Rest somewhere comfortable and take small sips of water if you can keep fluids down.
Follow-up: Have you vomited, or are you unable to keep fluids down?

# weakness
Weakness can have many causes, so what comes with it matters.
- Sit or lie down somewhere safe and rest. If you can drink normally, take some water.
Follow-up: Did the weakness start suddenly, or are you having trouble speaking, walking, breathing, or staying awake?

# fever
Checking your temperature can help you keep track of a fever and decide what to do next.
- Use the digital thermometer in the kit if available and follow its instructions.
- Rest and drink fluids regularly if you can.
Follow-up: Have you checked your temperature, and how long have you had the fever?

# sore throat
For an ordinary sore throat, simple supportive care is usually the first step.
- Rest your voice and drink water or warm fluids regularly.
- Warm salt-water gargling may help if you can gargle safely.
Follow-up: Are you having trouble swallowing or breathing?

# cough
A cough can have many causes, so focus on comfort while watching for breathing problems or other warning signs.
- Rest and drink fluids regularly.
Follow-up: Are you having trouble breathing, chest pain, or coughing up blood?

# stomach pain
Stomach pain can have many causes, so the next step depends on severity and what else is happening.
- Rest and take fluids as tolerated while monitoring how the pain changes.
Follow-up: How severe is the pain, and is it getting worse or coming with vomiting, fainting, or blood?

# diarrhea
Keeping hydrated is important when you have diarrhea.
- Drink fluids regularly and rest.
- Seek medical help if you cannot keep fluids down or you develop severe weakness, fainting, or blood in the stool.
Follow-up: Are you able to drink fluids normally?

# runny nose
A runny or stuffy nose is often managed with simple supportive care.
- Rest and drink fluids regularly.
Follow-up: Are you having trouble breathing?

# minor wound
For a minor cut or wound, clean it, control any bleeding, and protect it from contamination.
- Gently rinse the wound with clean running water.
- If it is bleeding, apply steady direct pressure with clean gauze or a clean cloth until the bleeding is controlled.
- Once bleeding is controlled, cover a small wound with a clean adhesive bandage or appropriate dressing.
Follow-up: Is the wound bleeding right now?

# severe bleeding
Heavy or uncontrolled bleeding is an emergency and should be treated as such.
- Call 911 and get a nearby teacher, school staff member, or other responsible adult immediately.
- Apply firm, continuous direct pressure with sterile gauze or a clean cloth.
- Keep pressure on the wound and do not repeatedly lift the dressing to check it.
- If blood soaks through, keep the original dressing in place and add more material on top while maintaining pressure.
Follow-up: Is the bleeding still heavy or not stopping despite continuous pressure?

# burn
For a minor thermal burn, cooling and protecting the area are the first priorities.
- Cool the burn under clean, cool running water as soon as possible.
- Do not apply ice directly to the skin and do not intentionally break blisters.
- After cooling, protect a minor burn with a sterile non-stick burn dressing from the kit.
- Serious, deep, extensive, electrical, chemical, facial, hand, joint, or genital burns need prompt medical assessment.
Follow-up: Where is the burn, and about how large or deep is it?

# injury
For an injury involving a bump, bruise, sprain, or strain, protect the area and watch for signs that it may be more serious.
- Stop the activity and rest the injured area.
- For a minor closed injury with swelling, use an instant cold pack wrapped in a cloth barrier according to the package directions.
- Do not force painful movement.
Follow-up: Can you move or walk normally, and is there severe pain, swelling, numbness, or deformity?

# sprain
A sprain can involve significant soft-tissue injury, so protect the area and watch for fracture warning signs.
- Stop using the injured joint and rest it.
- Use a cold pack with a cloth barrier for minor swelling.
- An elastic bandage can provide gentle support for a minor sprain; it should be snug, not tight.
- Do not force the joint to move if movement is very painful or the joint looks deformed.
Follow-up: Can you bear weight or move the joint normally?

# fracture
A suspected fracture should be treated as a broken bone until it can be medically assessed.
- Keep the injured area as still as possible and do not straighten or push the bone back into place.
- If there is an open wound, cover it with clean or sterile gauze and control bleeding with direct pressure around the wound; do not press directly on a protruding bone.
- If appropriate for a closed injury, a wrapped cold pack can help with swelling.
- Get urgent medical assessment. Call 911 for severe deformity, uncontrolled bleeding, numbness/coldness, shock, or an injury involving the head, neck, spine, pelvis, or upper leg.
Follow-up: Is the area deformed, unable to move, numb or cold, or is any bone visible?

# dislocation
A suspected dislocation needs medical assessment. Do not try to put the joint back into place yourself.
- Keep the joint in the position you found it and avoid unnecessary movement.
- Do not straighten, force, or “pop” the joint back into place.
- Support the injured area with a sling or other comfortable support if appropriate without forcing the joint.
- A wrapped cold pack may help with swelling. Seek urgent medical care, and call 911 for severe deformity, numbness/coldness, uncontrolled bleeding, shock, or a major injury.
Follow-up: Does the joint look deformed, or do you have numbness, tingling, or loss of circulation below it?

# nosebleed
For a nosebleed, positioning and steady pressure are the first steps.
- Sit upright and lean slightly forward.
- Pinch the soft part of the nose continuously for about 10–15 minutes without repeatedly checking.
- Spit out blood rather than swallowing it.
- Get urgent help if bleeding is heavy, follows major trauma, or does not stop with sustained pressure.
Follow-up: Is the bleeding heavy, or has it continued despite continuous pressure?

# eye issue
For minor eye irritation or loose debris, gentle irrigation is the safest first step.
- Rinse the eye gently with sterile saline or clean water.
- Do not rub the eye or dig into it with fingers, cotton swabs, or tweezers.
- If an object is embedded, vision changes, severe pain is present, or a chemical entered the eye, seek urgent medical care.
Follow-up: What got into the eye, and is your vision affected?

# chemical exposure
Chemical exposure can cause serious injury and needs prompt decontamination and medical guidance.
- Move away from the source and avoid exposing yourself to the chemical.
- For chemical exposure to skin, remove contaminated clothing and rinse the affected skin with plenty of running water.
- For chemical exposure to the eye, immediately flush with clean running water or sterile saline and seek urgent medical care.
- Call 911 or poison/emergency services for severe symptoms, breathing problems, major exposure, or an unknown dangerous chemical.
Follow-up: Did the chemical get on the skin, in the eyes, or get inhaled/swallowed?

# head injury
A head injury can be more serious than it first appears, especially after a strong impact.
- Stop activity and keep the person still if there is concern for a neck or spine injury.
- Do not ask them to move the neck or back if a spinal injury is suspected.
- Watch for worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion, unusual sleepiness, seizure, weakness, vision problems, or loss of consciousness.
- Get emergency help for severe or worsening symptoms or any signs of serious head, neck, or spinal injury.
Follow-up: Did they lose consciousness, vomit repeatedly, become confused, or have worsening headache, weakness, or unusual sleepiness?

# spinal injury
A possible head, neck, or spinal injury is an emergency-risk situation.
- Call 911 and get a nearby responsible adult immediately.
- Tell the person not to move and leave them in the position found unless you must move them for immediate safety, CPR, or uncontrolled bleeding.
- Do not remove a helmet unless needed to provide CPR.
- Keep monitoring breathing and responsiveness until help arrives.
Follow-up: Are they having trouble breathing, losing movement or sensation, or becoming less responsive?

# choking
Severe choking is an emergency. If the person cannot cough, speak, or breathe normally, act immediately.
- Call 911 and get a nearby adult or responder.
- If the person can cough forcefully, encourage coughing and watch closely.
- If an adult or child is conscious but cannot cough, speak, or breathe, give choking first aid according to your training or follow the 911 dispatcher’s instructions.
- If the person becomes unresponsive, begin CPR and follow dispatcher instructions; look for an object in the mouth only when opening the airway and remove it if clearly visible.
Follow-up: Can the person cough, speak, or breathe normally right now?

# seizure
During a seizure, protect the person from injury and do not restrain them or put anything in their mouth.
- Clear nearby hard or sharp objects and protect the head with something soft if possible.
- Do not hold the person down and do not put food, water, medicine, or objects in their mouth.
- Time the seizure if you can and stay with the person.
- Call 911 if the seizure lasts 5 minutes or longer, repeats without recovery, occurs in water, causes serious injury, involves breathing problems, or is a first known seizure.
Follow-up: Is the seizure still happening, or has the person become unresponsive or had trouble breathing?

# fainting
If someone faints, protect them from injury and check responsiveness and breathing.
- Help them lie down safely and check whether they respond and are breathing normally.
- If they are not responding and are not breathing normally or are only gasping, call 911 and start CPR.
- If they are breathing normally, keep monitoring them and do not let them stand suddenly.
Follow-up: Are they awake and breathing normally now?

# breathing
Trouble breathing can become an emergency quickly.
- Call 911 for severe or worsening breathing difficulty and get a nearby responsible adult immediately.
- Keep the person in a position that makes breathing as comfortable as possible.
- Do not leave them alone; follow the emergency dispatcher’s instructions.
Follow-up: Are they unable to speak normally, turning blue/gray, becoming confused, or getting worse quickly?

# chest pain
Chest pain can have serious causes and should not be ignored.
- Stop activity and sit or rest in a comfortable position.
- Call 911 for severe, new, pressure-like, worsening, or unexplained chest pain, especially with trouble breathing, sweating, fainting, nausea, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw, back, or shoulder.
- Do not drive yourself if emergency symptoms are present.
Follow-up: Is the chest pain severe, new, worsening, or happening with trouble breathing, sweating, fainting, or nausea?

# cpr
CPR is used when a person is unresponsive and not breathing normally or is only gasping.
- Make sure the scene is safe, then check responsiveness and normal breathing.
- Call 911 or send someone to call, and get an AED if one is available.
- For a teen or adult, place the heel of one hand in the center of the chest, put the other hand on top, and push hard and fast at 100–120 compressions per minute.
- Let the chest recoil after each compression and minimize interruptions.
- Turn on the AED as soon as it arrives and follow its voice prompts; continue CPR until the person moves/breathes normally or trained responders take over.
Follow-up: Is the person unresponsive and not breathing normally or only gasping right now?

# heat exhaustion
Heat illness can worsen quickly, especially if confusion or loss of consciousness develops.
- Move the person to a cooler place and stop physical activity.
- Loosen excess clothing and cool the body with cool wet cloths or other safe cooling methods.
- If fully awake and able to swallow normally, give small amounts of cool water.
- Call 911 immediately for confusion, fainting, seizures, inability to drink, or signs of severe heat illness.
Follow-up: Are they confused, fainting, vomiting repeatedly, or unable to drink normally?

# cold exposure
Cold exposure can become dangerous when the person is confused, very drowsy, or shivering uncontrollably.
- Move the person to a warmer, dry environment and remove wet clothing if possible.
- Wrap them in dry layers or the emergency thermal blanket from the kit.
- If fully awake and able to swallow, offer a warm non-alcoholic drink.
- Call 911 for confusion, loss of consciousness, severe weakness, or abnormal breathing.
Follow-up: Are they confused, unusually drowsy, or having abnormal breathing?

# allergy
An allergic reaction can range from mild symptoms to a life-threatening emergency.
- Move away from the suspected trigger if it is safe to do so.
- Watch closely for swelling of the lips or tongue, trouble breathing, wheezing, fainting, or rapidly worsening symptoms.
- Call 911 immediately for breathing difficulty, throat/tongue swelling, fainting, or other signs of a severe reaction.
- Do not delay emergency care while trying to identify the exact cause.
Follow-up: Are there any breathing problems, throat or tongue swelling, fainting, or rapidly worsening symptoms?

# palpitations
A racing or pounding heartbeat can have several causes and should be assessed in context.
- Stop exertion and sit or rest.
- Seek emergency help if the racing heartbeat is accompanied by chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting, or severe dizziness.
Follow-up: Did it start suddenly, and are you having chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, or severe dizziness?

# confusion
Sudden confusion can be a medical emergency, especially when it is new or accompanied by weakness or speech problems.
- Stay with the person and keep them in a safe place.
- Call 911 for sudden or severe confusion, especially with weakness, facial drooping, trouble speaking, severe headache, fainting, or trouble breathing.
Follow-up: Did the confusion start suddenly, or are there any problems with speech, movement, breathing, or consciousness?

# anxiety
Stress or anxiety can feel intense even when there is no immediate danger.
- Move somewhere safe and slow things down.
- Take slow, comfortable breaths and focus on one manageable step at a time.
- If symptoms are severe, new, or feel medically different from usual, seek appropriate medical help.
Follow-up: Do you feel physically safe right now?

# fatigue
Fatigue can have many causes, including lack of rest, stress, or illness.
- Take a break, rest, and drink some water if you can.
Follow-up: How long have you been feeling unusually tired?

# sleepiness
Ordinary sleepiness can come from lack of rest, while unusual or severe drowsiness needs more context.
- If you are simply tired, rest somewhere safe and give yourself a chance to recover.
Follow-up: Did you get enough sleep, and is the sleepiness unusual for you?

# skin rash
A rash can have many causes, so avoid assuming what caused it from the description alone.
- Avoid any new product or obvious irritant that seems to make it worse and monitor how it changes.
- Seek urgent help if the rash is rapidly spreading with breathing difficulty, facial/tongue swelling, fainting, or severe illness.
Follow-up: When did the rash start, and are you having swelling or trouble breathing?
